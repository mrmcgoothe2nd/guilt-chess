How guilt works

Everything is regular chess, with one change: taking a piece weighs on the piece that did it.

  • Capturing makes a piece guilty. It gets a red ring. It can still move to empty squares, block, and be captured, but it can’t capture anything.
  • Guilty pieces don’t give check. A piece that can’t capture can’t threaten the king, so your king may walk right past it.
  • Bishops grant absolution. When you move a bishop, every guilty piece of yours on the eight squares around where it lands is forgiven. The bishop has to make the move; walking a guilty piece up to a bishop doesn’t count.
  • Bishops can’t forgive themselves. A bishop that captures needs your other bishop to clear it. Lose both and every capture is permanent.
  • The fine print. En passant counts as a capture. A promoted pawn keeps its guilt, and promoting to a bishop doesn’t absolve anyone that turn. Kings can never stand next to each other.

The game ends in checkmate, stalemate, resignation, threefold repetition, 50 moves without a pawn move or capture, or when only the kings are left.
